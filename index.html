How the simulation works

  • The day's UV follows a bell curve fitted to ARPANSA's minute-by-minute measurements for the city and month, 2016 to 2025. "Typical day" uses the median daily peak, "Clear day" the 90th percentile.
  • One UV index unit is 0.025 W/m² of sunburn-weighted UV. Dose builds every 5 minutes and is divided by your SPF at that moment.
  • A sunburn is one minimal erythema dose (MED), which depends on skin type.
  • Protection above bare skin halves every few hours: about 6.5 hours with swimming and activity, 8 with sweating and 24 at rest. Reapplying restores it.
  • Each simulated person varies in how much they apply, how their bottle performs and how sensitive their skin is, and gets one of the two dose models at random. The same 1,000 people are reused for every setting, so changes come from your choices, not chance.

Sources

  • UV data: ARPANSA Ultraviolet Radiation Index via data.gov.au, CC BY 2.5 AU. Hobart uses the Kingston station.
  • Sunburn thresholds by skin type: Fajuyigbe and Young (2016). Types V and VI are poorly studied.
  • Amount people apply: Autier et al. (2001), median 0.39 mg/cm²; review by Petersen and Wulf (2014).
  • SPF and thickness: exponential, Faurschou and Wulf (2007) and Schalka et al. (2009); linear, Bimczok et al. (2007) and Ou-Yang et al. (2012).
  • Wear-off: Bodekær et al. (2008) and Ruvolo et al. (2020). Two small studies, so treat these as rough.
  • How far a bottle strays from its label is our assumption. CHOICE's June 2025 tests found 16 of 20 SPF 50 products below their label.
  • Test amount: ISO 24444 (2 mg/cm²). 7 teaspoons per adult body and reapplying every 2 hours: Cancer Council Australia.
  • This is a teaching model, not sun safety advice. Follow the SunSmart UV alert and see a doctor about skin concerns.
